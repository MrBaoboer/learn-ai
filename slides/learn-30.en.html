Why a short phrase is enough

A gray quote bar sits above the box and will not close. It says this page plus its title is already attached. The page body goes to her on its own. So type “Lost me here”. That “here” has a landing spot; she will not ask which page you are on.

This changes where a question starts. The first sentence used to introduce the material; the second got to the doubt. Now the first sentence can ask about the real stuck spot. The page is already the background. You only add your question.

To point more tightly, mark a stretch in the body. Select two or more characters and an overlay appears. Tap “Ask Alice,” the stretch enters a quote chip, and the question is that stretch. Use it when you know the stuck sentence. When you only know the whole page got away from you, asking in the box is enough.

Which layers copy-paste drops

The step you skip is not only convenience. When you paste, it is easy to paste only the middle sentence and leave the one above on the page. She only sees the block you handed over, so she turns the waiting period into a dictionary line: a stretch after the policy starts, during which a diagnosis is not covered. The exception in the sentence above — a diagnosis from accidental injury is not bound by the waiting period — she never gets a chance to mention.

The demo contrast is the same point. Ask on this page, and Alice gets the page, the body, the selection, and a short hint pinning “here” to this page. Haul one sentence into another chat, and all that remains is the block you pasted. The input looks longer; the background is thinner. The answer drifts into a generic gloss.
