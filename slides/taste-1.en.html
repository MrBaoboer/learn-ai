Scarcity moved

In the past, "can build" was valuable: knowing code, design tools, video editing—the tool itself was the barrier. AI flattened that barrier. Usable stuff is everywhere. What's scarce now is building it right, with taste: ten options in front of you, pick the right one, say what's off and how to fix it.

Art owns expression; design owns communication

By now you always hit the same pushback: taste is talent, beauty is in the eye of the beholder—how do you teach that? Mullet and Sano (Kevin Mullet & Darrell Sano) untangle it in Designing Visual Interfaces. Art is how artists express themselves—fewer constraints the better, uniqueness pays. Design makes something for a specific audience with a specific use; the top job is clear communication.

"Design is concerned with finding the most appropriate representation for communicating certain specific information."
Mullet and Sano, Designing Visual Interfaces

That sentence is the foundation of this Part. Judging art asks "what does it express?"—no right answer; you can argue till dawn. Judging design asks "did the message reach the target audience?"—that you can verify.

Cooper calls this path goal-oriented in About Face 4, and adds: visual communication always carries some subjective judgment, but once experience and business goals are clear, taste gaps can be narrowed by effort. Aesthetics isn't kicked out—it's framed by goals.

Key Takeaways
✓

The barrier to shipping collapsed: anyone can make something usable in ten minutes—"usable" is devaluing.

✓

Scarcity moved: from "can build" to "build it right, with taste." Picking the right one from a pile becomes the human's core job.

✓

Aesthetic judgment has a referee: art owns expression, design owns communication (Mullet and Sano). Once goals are clear, taste fights narrow into verifiable questions—the theoretical base for "taste can be trained."
