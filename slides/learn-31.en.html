You just read this page from top to bottom. The title made sense, the paragraphs connected, and none of Alice’s notes stopped you. Your mouse is already heading for the next page, and you’ve already told yourself: I’ve got this one.

Don’t flip yet. Try answering three details without looking back: where the mock interview opens, how many questions she asks at a time, and what she does first when you drift. Those lines felt familiar a minute ago. Saying them yourself may not come as fast.

Two states are mixed together here. Following along means that while the words are still on the screen, you can recognize what they say. Retrieving means that after the words leave your eyes, you can still say empty-state prompt five is the “mock interview”, and that she asks only one question at a time.

Following along is not proof you can retrieve it.The page keeps handing you the words. Reading them again only makes them more familiar. You still have no result that points to which sentence you can say yourself, and which one you only nodded at while it was in front of you.

The way to split those two states is to let the question appear first, then see whether you can pull the answer out of your head. If you can answer, you have something to remember. If you drift, the drift shows up. If you can’t retrieve a single sentence, you also know which stretch to go back to.

So the direction has to flip. While you read, Alice talks and you receive. While she quizzes you, you talk and she points.She doesn’t smooth the answer for you. She waits for you to say it.The demo below puts the right, drifted, and “I don’t know” paths on the same question. Click each one.

Why hearing it again still can’t tell you which sentence you know

When you stall, the cheapest move is to let her say it again. The same stretch comes back in different words, and you’re still reading. She can keep rephrasing forever, and this path never tells you whether you know it.

Letting AI quiz you right now flips the direction: you talk.If you’re right, you go straight to the next one. If you drift she probes first, pointing at the drift: nothing in that answer was false — it was written for whose situation?She only gives a short answer when you say you don’t know.

That probe beat matters. She doesn’t drop the full answer on top of yours. She shrinks the gap to one sentence. You have to say it again before you can tell whether you were missing a concept, a cause, or the step of putting this page’s judgment into your own scene.

The right side of the demo leaves two records: what you retrieved, and what you still need to go back over. The first shows what can already come out of your head. The second gives the next review a starting point.The value of one quiz is splitting what you know from what only looks familiar.
