What each line is for

Line 1 is the next message you send to AI as-is: what you already know, what you need to settle this time, how it should quiz you, and where you’re most likely to miss. These four parts don’t have to look tidy. AI just needs to know where to catch you.

Line 2 is a real passage in your hands that you still can’t read. Have AI list what this stretch covers, point at the word blocking you, fill only that layer, then go back to the source and say it in your own words. When you write this line, at least put the material’s name and the stall, so you know where to start today.

Line 3 is a result you can check. Give it a time, an audience, and a visible action — for example, “By Wednesday, circle three places in the contract that work against me, and be able to tell HR the grounds.” If a week later you can tell whether you did it, this line is finished.

How to use these three lines this week

Stitched together, the three lines are a checklist. Copy line 1 into the next question as-is. Run line 2 today, and write that jargon-free sentence next to the material. Stick line 3 where you can see it, and check it at the time you wrote.
