            When the model predicts the next token, every token in the current context influences the probability distribution.
            The constraint words injected via System Prompt act as "high-quality prior context":
            they raise the probability of the "I'm not sure" sequence and lower the probability of fabrication sequences.
            The essence: use carefully crafted prior context to directionally bias the probability distribution of subsequent output.
          

        

        

          
⚠ Limitation: The model doesn't know what it doesn't know

          

            

              
When constraint instructions work

              

                When the model has some uncertainty about a question, constraint words can push it toward admitting it doesn't know.

                → Works for: questions beyond knowledge scope, ambiguous queries, time-sensitive information
              

            

            

            

              
When constraint instructions fail

              

                When the model is highly confident in a wrong answer, its top-ranked token is already wrong, and constraint words cannot affect that probability.

                → Fails for: systematic errors in training data, outdated knowledge treated as fact
              

            

            

            

              
PM Countermeasure

              

                For domains where "the model is highly confident but may be wrong," Prompt constraints alone are insufficient.

                → Combine with RAG (inject real knowledge) or human review as fallback
