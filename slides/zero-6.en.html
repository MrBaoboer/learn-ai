Chapter Zero · What You Can Safely Hand to It

Which Jobs You Can Hand Over with Confidence, and Which Deserve One Extra Check

You already know it's remarkably capable — and that it makes mistakes with a straight face. One key question remains: faced with a concrete task, how do you decide in three seconds whether to hand it to AI? Play these 8 quick calls first — the rule is hiding in your own answers.

The judgment game · How would you treat the AI's answer here?
The rule revealed · Ask yourself two questions first
①

Does this have "one correct answer"?

Things with no correct answer (names, copy, ideas, checklists): it gives you 10, you pick 1 — a miss isn't even a mistake. Use freely. Things with one correct answer (numbers, dates, legal text, facts): its memories can blur — worth a second look.

②

If the answer is wrong, how bad is it?

Bad copy just gets rewritten — low stakes, experiment away. But for anything touching health, money, or the law, one mistake may be unrecoverable — treat AI's word as reference, and checking with an authoritative source or a professional before deciding is the steadier move.

One picture to remember · The AI-trust quadrants
✅ Use freely

No correct answer + you can judge the result yourself

Naming, polishing, gist translation, brainstorming, checklists, first drafts. Its output is just "candidates" — you're the referee.

💡 Ask freely, verify before acting

Knowledge that helps you understand

Explaining concepts, decoding reports, walking through how things work. Using it to "get it" is peak efficiency; but before a decision rides on it, check the sources.

⚠️ Verify

Concrete facts with one correct answer

Numbers, dates, names, legal text, time-sensitive info. A quick search confirms it in seconds — well worth the ten-odd seconds.

🚨 Reference only

Health · Money · Law

It can help you understand what the doctor said or what a contract clause means — but which medication to take, whether to sign, whether to invest: those go to the professionals.

✅ What this lesson wants to share with you

  • Start with two little questions: does this have one correct answer? How bad is it if the answer is wrong?
  • For things with no correct answer, it's a great helper: it offers extra options, and the final pick is yours
  • Concrete numbers and dates are worth a quick search: ten-odd seconds buys real peace of mind
  • Health, money, and the law: AI can help you understand — leaving the final call to professionals is the steadier way
