Every Token generation step, the model first computes logits for candidate tokens, then applies softmax to get a probability distribution. Temperature scales logits proportionally before softmax: smaller T widens the gap between values, sharpening the distribution; larger T narrows the gap, flattening it. This is the most direct way to intervene in per-step sampling randomness, but it cannot distinguish which steps should be conservative and which can be creative. That's why factual Q&A needs a consistently low temperature, while creative scenarios can open it up.
⚠ Limitation: Lowering the parameter does NOT eliminate hallucinations
Low temperature locks in "most likely" — most likely ≠ most correct
T=0 only makes the model pick the highest-probability token each step. But if the training data had an incorrect answer to that question, the highest-probability token is still the wrong answer — and the output will be consistently, confidently wrong
Affects randomness only — not the knowledge boundary
Temperature controls "how to sample each step," but cannot inject knowledge the model doesn't have: things the model doesn't know, it will still fabricate at low temperature — just more consistently
PM Mitigation Strategy
Parameter tuning is the cheapest first line of defense, but you can't rely on it alone. For scenarios with high factual accuracy requirements, you must pair it with RAG or human review
